## 摘要
全球供应链日益暴露于贸易政策冲击以及共同地缘政治事件引发的物理中断。我们构建了一个具有两个决策阶段的稳健优化框架，用于战略性供应链重构。供应商资质认定与运输弧启用决策在不确定性实现之前作出，而运营流量随后进行自适应调整。地缘政治不确定性通过国家层面冲击来表示，这些冲击能够共同触发关税升级、政策引致的跨境运力限制、设施中断以及物理运输故障。由此产生的 min-max-min 问题通过对运营补救问题取对偶进行重构，得到一个非凸混合整数双线性对抗问题，并在列与约束生成过程中求解。与完全枚举的比较在小规模算例上验证了实现，而在 60 个不同规模的合成供应链网络上的实验评估了计算性能。结果表明，在战略设计阶段忽略地缘政治传导渠道，可能在全面地缘政治压力下导致成本和服务显著恶化。更广泛的保护主要通过运输网络配置的变化实现，而非通过增加供应商资质认定。稳健性能还取决于地缘政治后果的严重程度与构成；在测试范围内，跨境运力限制比关税严重程度可比的比例变化产生更强影响。基于地理的案例分析进一步表明，各个不确定性渠道的价值取决于网络结构和地理暴露程度。

## Abstract
Global supply chains are increasingly exposed to trade policy shocks and physical disruptions arising from common geopolitical events. We develop a robust optimization framework with two decision stages for strategic supply chain reconfiguration. Supplier qualification and transportation arc activation decisions are made before uncertainty is realized, while operational flows adapt afterward. Geopolitical uncertainty is represented through country level shocks that can jointly trigger tariff escalation, policy induced restrictions on cross border capacity, facility disruptions, and physical transportation failures. The resulting min max min problem is reformulated by dualizing the operational recourse problem, yielding a nonconvex mixed integer bilinear adversarial problem solved within a column and constraint generation procedure. Comparison with complete enumeration verifies the implementation on small instances, while experiments on 60 synthetic supply chain networks of varying sizes assess computational performance. The results show that omitting geopolitical transmission channels during strategic design can lead to substantial deterioration in cost and service under full geopolitical stress. Broader protection is achieved primarily through changes in transportation network configuration rather than increased supplier qualification. Robust performance also depends on the severity and composition of geopolitical consequences, with restrictions on cross border capacity exerting a stronger effect than comparable proportional changes in tariff severity over the tested ranges. A geographically grounded case analysis further shows that the value of individual uncertainty channels depends on network structure and geographic exposure.
