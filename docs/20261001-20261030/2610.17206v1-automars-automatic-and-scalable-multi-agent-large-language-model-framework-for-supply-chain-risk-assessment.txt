A B S T R A C T
We propose AutoMARS (Automatic Multi-Agent Risk Assessment), an agentic framework for
automatic and scalable comparative risk assessment in supply chain networks. The framework
enables end-to-end execution of analytical workflows by automatically planning tasks, coordi-
nating specialized agents, invoking external analytical tools, and synthesizing domain-specific
results into integrated risk evaluations. AutoMARS further supports scalable risk comparison
across multiple locations by parallelizing independent analytical tasks. The framework adopts
a multi-domain, multi-agent, and multi-tool architecture that integrates heterogeneous risk
factors from socioeconomic, disaster, environmental, and transportation domains. Through
coordinated agent interactions and tool integration, AutoMARS enables structured, systematic,
and interpretable comparative analysis to support supply chain risk-aware location evaluation.
1. Introduction
Modern supply chains operate in increasingly volatile, uncertain, complex, and ambiguous (VUCA) environments.
In such environments, companies increasingly depend on geographically dispersed suppliers, complex logistics net-
works, and digitally coordinated operations to sustain production and distribution activities. While these configurations
improve efficiency and global reach, they also expand the exposure of supply chains to diverse disruption sources.
Events such as natural hazards, environmental disruptions, transportation accidents, and socioeconomic vulnerabilities
can interrupt material flows, delay logistics operations, and create instability across multiple stages of supply chains.
Consequently, managing disruption risk has become a critical challenge for organizations seeking to maintain supply
continuity and operational resilience in increasingly uncertain environments (Ivanov, 2022; Katsaliaki et al., 2022).
To address the above challenge, extensive research has focused on developing analytical frameworks and decision-
support methods for assessing and managing risks in interconnected supply chain networks (Montreuil, 2011;
Montreuil et al., 2012). Existing studies have explored various approaches to support risk identification, evaluation,
and mitigation in complex supply chain environments, including optimization-based models (Ivanov et al., 2018),
simulation methods (Cerabona et al., 2024), and multi-agent coordination mechanisms (Giannakis and Louis, 2011).
These approaches aim to improve the ability of supply chain systems to anticipate disruptions, evaluate potential
impacts, and support informed operational and strategic decision-making. More recently, advances in artificial
intelligence (AI) and large language models (LLMs) have opened new opportunities for enhancing risk assessment
capabilities in complex supply networks. For example, Quan et al. (2026) propose a novel LLM-enhanced framework,
MARS, that integrates both structured and unstructured factors to support risk-aware logistic hub site selection.
Despite the advantages of existing approaches, several key limitations remain. First, fully integrated end-to-
end automation is still limited, as many multi-agent systems (MAS) require partial human intervention and manual
coordination across analytical steps (Ambati, 2025), preventing seamless execution from task decomposition to final
assessment. Second, scalability remains a challenge in large-scale risk assessment scenarios (Katsaliaki et al., 2022),
where sequential interactions with data sources and analytical agents lead to significant computational overhead,

In this paper, we propose AutoMARS (Automatic Multi-Agent Risk Assessment), an agentic framework designed
to support automatic and scalable comparative risk assessment in supply chain networks. The framework enables
end-to-end execution of the analytical workflow, allowing a user to submit a high-level request while the system
automatically plans and coordinates multiple specialized agents to invoke external tools, perform domain-specific
analyses, and synthesize the results into an integrated risk evaluation. By integrating multi-domain information,
coordinating multiple agents, and invoking diverse analytical tools, AutoMARS provides a unified framework for
complex risk analysis tasks. AutoMARS further supports scalable analysis across multiple locations by enabling
parallel execution of independent analytical tasks. In addition, the framework emphasizes comparative risk evaluation
across locations, enabling more consistent and reliable decision-making by focusing on relative risk ordering rather
than absolute scores. This comparative perspective provides a more robust and interpretable basis for decision-making
in supply chain location evaluation. Risk-conscious logistic location selection is essential for facility location and
network design decisions, including hub siting and infrastructure investment. Overall, this work aims to develop an
automatic, scalable, and reliable framework for comparative supply chain risk assessment across multiple locations.
In this study, we aim to address the following research questions (RQs):
• RQ1: How to enable end-to-end automation in supply chain risk assessment?
• RQ2: How to scale supply chain risk assessment while mitigating the computational complexity introduced by
multi-agent coordination?
• RQ3: How to conduct comparative risk assessment across multiple locations?
Answering these research questions, our main contributions in this paper are as follows:
1. End-to-end automatic framework for supply chain risk assessment: We propose AutoMARS, an automatic
multi-agent framework that enables end-to-end execution of supply chain risk assessment tasks. Given a high-
level user request, the framework automatically plans and decomposes the analytical workflow, coordinates
specialized agents, invokes relevant external tools, and synthesizes domain-specific results into a unified risk
evaluation.
2. Scalable risk comparison through parallel execution: The proposed framework enables scalable comparative
risk assessment across multiple locations by executing independent analytical tasks in parallel. This design
significantly reduces computational time when evaluating many locations and supports efficient risk comparison
in large-scale supply chain decision contexts.
3. Multi-domain risk analysis through multi-agent multi-tool integration: The framework integrates heteroge-
neous risk factors across multiple domains, including socioeconomic, disaster, environmental, and transportation
domains, by coordinating specialized agents and invoking diverse external analytical tools. This multi-domain,
multi-agent, and multi-tool architecture enables comprehensive risk analysis by combining information from
multiple sources and analytical perspectives.

2.1. Automation in Supply Chain Risk Management
SCRM involves continuous tasks such as risk identification, assessment, monitoring, and response (Fan et al.,
2017), which are difficult to sustain under rapidly changing conditions. In practice, risk assessment often relies on
expert judgment and static analyses, limiting scalability across regions and time (Sodhi et al., 2012; Ho et al., 2015).
Meanwhile, risk information arises from heterogeneous, high-frequency sources, creating the need for automated
processing and timely updates (Chu et al., 2020). This motivates artificial intelligence (AI)-driven automation in SCRM
to improve efficiency, consistency, and scalability (Ganesh and Kalpana, 2022; Ziari and Taleizadeh, 2025; Chowdhury
et al., 2026).
Existing automation approaches support the representation and modeling of temporal events from text, enabling
standardized analysis of evolving risks (Liu and Quan, 2024, 2025b; Liu et al., 2025). They also integrate multi-source
data for risk assessment and decision support in logistics contexts (Zhao et al., 2024; Quan et al., 2025b). More broadly,
generative AI and automated decision systems enhance analytical capabilities and responsiveness (Oger et al., 2018;
Sharma, 2022; Jackson et al., 2024), with applications in areas such as inventory management demonstrating efficiency
gains (Saleem, 2020).
Despite these advances, automation in SCRM remains fragmented, with most approaches focusing on isolated
functions rather than end-to-end workflows (Das and Perona, 2025) and offering limited support for systematic risk
comparison across regions (Katsaliaki et al., 2022). Simulation-based and digital twin systems enable automated
monitoring but rely on predefined models, limiting adaptability (Ivanov and Dolgui, 2021). Evidence from automated
multi-agent systems (MAS) in other domains further suggests the need for more integrated frameworks (Liu, 2025).
Overall, automation enhances efficiency, consistency, and responsiveness in SCRM by enabling data integration and
supporting coordinated risk assessment.
2.2. Automated LLM-Based Multi-Agent Orchestration
Language interaction enables flexible coordination among decision-making entities in complex systems. Tradi-
tional MAS rely on predefined protocols, limiting adaptability to dynamic contexts and heterogeneous information. In
contrast, LLM-based agents use natural language to coordinate roles, enabling orchestration through conversational
workflows, role assignment, and task decomposition (Li et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2024; Hong et al., 2023; Guo et al.,
2024).
Existing work spans domain-specific applications and general-purpose frameworks. Domain-oriented systems use
LLM-driven agents coordinated through automated workflows for tasks such as inventory management and customer-
facing processes (Quan and Liu, 2024b; Quan et al., 2025a). General approaches orchestrate multiple agents or tools
via task decomposition to solve complex problems end to end (Shen et al., 2023; Schick et al., 2023).
In practice, multi-agent coordination is enabled at multiple levels. Frameworks such as LangChain1 and Lang-
Graph2 support structured orchestration through pipeline- or graph-based workflows, while protocols such as Model
Context Protocol3 (MCP) and Agent2Agent Protocol4 (A2A) standardize agent interoperability and tool integration.
Building on these foundations, systems such as BMAD5, GSD6, and OpenCLAW7 further demonstrate specification-
driven workflows and structured multi-agent orchestration.
Despite recent progress, LLM-based multi-agent orchestration faces challenges in stability and evaluation (Li et al.,
2024b; Guo et al., 2024; Tran et al., 2025). Ensuring alignment, safety, and controllability in coordinated workflows
remains critical for reliable deployment (Bai et al., 2022). Overall, automated multi-agent orchestration enables flexible
coordination, task decomposition, and scalable reasoning across agents and tools, but further advances are required to
ensure robustness, stability, and well-governed execution in real-world settings.

3. Problem Definition
Logistic location selection is a critical decision in supply chain networks, where evaluating and comparing risk
levels across different geographic regions plays an important role in ensuring operational stability and resilience.
Regions may differ significantly in terms of socioeconomic conditions, disaster exposure, environmental quality,
and transportation safety, all of which can affect the reliability of supply chain operations. However, previous risk
assessment approaches often require manual coordination across multiple analytical steps, provide limited capability
to integrate heterogeneous risk information from multiple domains, and require substantial computational time when
evaluating risks across many regions. These limitations restrict the ability to conduct efficient and comprehensive
comparative risk assessment across regions, particularly when a large number of locations must be evaluated. As
supply chain decision-making increasingly requires integrating heterogeneous risk information and comparing risks
across multiple regions, a key challenge is to develop frameworks that can automatically coordinate multi-domain
analyses, support parallel task execution, and efficiently synthesize risk indicators from diverse information sources.
This highlights the need for intelligent systems capable of planning tasks, coordinating multiple analytical components,
and synthesizing diverse forms of risk information to enable comprehensive and adaptive location risk evaluation.
Problem Description: Consider a set of candidate locations within a supply chain network. For each location, risk
information can be obtained from multiple domains. To analyze these heterogeneous risk indicators, a set of specialized
domain agents is available, where each agent is designed to handle a specific analytical task. Each agent can access
a collection of external tools that provide domain-specific data retrieval, processing, and analysis capabilities. The
objective is to enable Manager Agent to automatically plan and decompose the overall risk assessment workflow,
coordinate the execution of Domain Agents and their associated tools, and integrate multi-domain information into
a unified risk evaluation for each location. Reflection Agent evaluates the synthesized results and provides feedback,
allowing Manager Agent to revise the analysis until a consistent comparative risk assessment is obtained. To further
improve scalability, independent analytical tasks within the workflow can be executed in parallel.
In summary, the problem of comparative supply chain risk assessment requires a framework capable of coordinating
domain-specific analyses and integrating heterogeneous risk information across multiple locations. To address
this challenge, we propose an automatic multi-agent framework that supports dynamic task orchestration, parallel
execution, and iterative reflection for scalable and adaptive risk evaluation.

4.1. Framework
AutoMARS is an automatic multi-agent framework for comparative risk assessment across multiple locations in
supply chain networks. To enable end-to-end automation (RQ1), it supports coordinated planning, tool invocation,
and cross-domain synthesis within a unified workflow. As illustrated in Figure 1, the framework adopts a modular
architecture consisting of a Manager Agent, multiple Domain Agents with associated external tools, and a Reflection
Agent. Manager Agent plans and orchestrates the overall assessment workflow, decomposing the task into domain-
specific analyses and coordinating Domain Agents, which invoke external tools to retrieve and process heterogeneous
risk information. The results are then integrated into a comparative risk evaluation. Reflection Agent subsequently
evaluates the integrated results to verify the completeness and consistency of the reasoning process. If deficiencies are
identified, feedback is returned to Manager Agent, which revises the assessment accordingly. This iterative interaction
continues until a stable and reliable comparative risk assessment is obtained. The detailed workflow of AutoMARS

Prompt for Manager Agent (Condensed Version)
You are Manager Agent responsible for orchestrating multiple Domain Agents to perform a comparative risk assessment
between Location A and Location B. Your task is to plan, execute, and integrate the analytical workflow. Domain Agents
provide domain-specific evidence, while you control the workflow and decision logic.
Stage 1: Initial Planning. At the beginning of the workflow, Manager Agent constructs an initial analysis plan before
consulting any Domain Agent. The plan specifies the domains to be analyzed, identifies which Domain Agents should be
activated, and determines which analyses can be executed in parallel. It also defines the role of each Domain Agent and
outlines how evidence from multiple domains will be integrated to support the final comparative risk judgment.
Stage 2: Evidence-Guided Execution. Manager Agent coordinates the execution of Domain Agents and collects
domain-specific evidence as analyses are completed. During this process, Manager Agent summarizes the obtained results,
evaluates whether the current plan remains appropriate, and revises the execution strategy when new evidence suggests
that adjustments are necessary.

result to Reflection Agent for validation. If Reflection Agent identifies deficiencies, Manager Agent revises the comparative
assessment according to the feedback while preserving previously generated domain evidence. If additional domain
analyses are required, updated evidence is incorporated into the synthesis. This revision loop continues until Reflection
Agent accepts the final assessment.

4.3. Efficient Execution
This section presents the key mechanisms that enable efficient execution in AutoMARS, including parallel
execution for reducing latency, caching mechanisms for avoiding redundant computation, and a comparison-based
sorting algorithm for scalable ranking across locations.
4.3.1. Parallel Execution
AutoMARS adopts a hierarchical parallel execution strategy to support efficient and scalable execution of multiple
independent tasks (RQ2). The system is implemented as a graph-based execution model in which Manager Agent
dynamically dispatches multiple Domain Agents as independent worker subgraphs that can execute concurrently.
Within each subgraph, tool calls are asynchronously executed through a dedicated tool execution module to support
parallel data retrieval from heterogeneous external sources. Intermediate outputs are stored in structured shared states
indexed by unique keys and merged through synchronized associative updates before being passed back to Manager
Agent for integration. By enabling parallel processing at both inter-agent and intra-agent levels, the system minimizes
idle waiting time while maintaining modularity and supporting scalable extension to additional domains and data
sources. This fan-out and fan-in execution pattern enables efficient multi-level parallel processing while ensuring
deterministic aggregation of multi-domain evidence.
4.3.2. Caching Mechanism
AutoMARS incorporates a caching mechanism to reduce redundant computation and improve efficiency in repeated
workflows involving external data retrieval and large language model (LLM) inference. The caching mechanism is
implemented through multiple cache layers tailored to different system components. For external tools, the system
maintains a disk-based cache in which final structured outputs are stored as persistent JSON files indexed by
deterministic identifiers constructed from input locations. Upon receiving a request, the system performs a lookup
using the corresponding identifier; matching entries are directly reused, while missing entries trigger new execution
and storage for future reuse. For LLM calls, caching is handled separately through a key-based mechanism in which
prompts and contextual inputs are normalized and serialized into a canonical representation before generating a hash-
based cache key. Upon each invocation, the system computes the corresponding key and checks for its existence in the
cache; matching keys enable direct reuse of previous responses and avoid repeated model inference. Together, these
mechanisms enable efficient and consistent reuse of both external data retrieval and repeated LLM computation.
4.3.3. Sorting Algorithm
To rank locations by overall risk, AutoMARS adopts a comparison-based sorting procedure in which ordering
decisions are determined through pairwise comparative risk assessments rather than predefined scalar scores. For
each location pair, the system invokes AutoMARS graph to generate a structured comparison result identifying the
higher-risk location, which is then mapped to an ordering relation used by the sorting algorithm. This design derives
ranking decisions directly from cross-domain evidence synthesized during multi-agent analysis, avoiding fixed global
weighting schemes while supporting interpretable comparative risk ranking. The underlying sorting algorithm follows
a stable and adaptive strategy based on Timsort21, which combines merge-based and insertion-based techniques to
efficiently handle partially ordered data, achieving a time complexity of 𝑂(𝑛log 𝑛) in the average and worst cases and
𝑂(𝑛) in the best case (Auger et al., 2018).

This section evaluates AutoMARS in terms of effectiveness, stability, and scalability for comparative risk

5.1. Experimental Setup
Geographical Scope. We conduct experiments on counties within Atlanta metropolitan area at multiple spatial
scales, including Atlanta Urban Core, Atlanta Regional Commission (ARC) region, and Metro Atlanta. Atlanta Urban
Core is defined as a five-county subset representing the most densely populated and economically active areas. ARC

Alpharetta Metropolitan Statistical Area, consisting of 29 counties. This multi-scale geographical design enables
systematic evaluation of the proposed framework across different levels of spatial aggregation.
Model Configuration. All agents in AutoMARS are implemented as large language model (LLM)-driven modules
using GPT-522 as the underlying reasoning engine. The system uses a temperature value of 1 during response
generation. All interactions with the model are conducted through API-based invocation with standardized prompts
and input formats.
Framework Implementation. AutoMARS is implemented using LangGraph to support graph-based multi-
agent orchestration. LangGraph provides the workflow structure for coordinating Manager Agent, Domain Agents,
and Reflection Agent, while maintaining shared state across execution steps. It also supports parallel execution of
independent agent branches and iterative routing for reflection-based revision.

6. Conclusion
In this paper, we propose AutoMARS, an automatic multi-agent framework for scalable and comparative supply
chain risk assessment. The framework enables end-to-end execution of analytical workflows by coordinating multiple
specialized agents, integrating heterogeneous risk information across domains, and invoking external analytical
tools. By combining structured task planning, multi-agent orchestration, and reflection-based validation, AutoMARS
provides a systematic and interpretable approach to comparative risk evaluation. In addition, the framework supports
parallel execution and a comparison-based sorting mechanism, enabling efficient analysis across multiple locations
without relying on predefined scoring schemes.
Experimental results demonstrate effectiveness, robustness, and scalability of the proposed framework. Pairwise
case study illustrates the ability of AutoMARS to synthesize cross-domain evidence and produces consistent
comparative assessments. Stability analyses show that the framework generates reliable results across repeated runs,
with strong consistency in both pairwise comparisons and ranking outcomes. Furthermore, ranking experiments across
multiple spatial scales reveal that AutoMARS maintains stable ordering structures, with variability primarily limited
to locations with similar risk profiles. Although adjacent counties appear geographically continuous, the observed red-
green differences reflect underlying heterogeneity captured by more than a dozen indicators across socioeconomic,
disaster, environmental, and traffic domains. Variations in population scale, vulnerability, economic structure, and
exposure conditions can lead to substantial differences in risk even between neighboring counties. These boundaries
Y. Quan
Page 17 of 20


therefore represent aggregated multi-dimensional differences rather than isolated factors. Nevertheless, because the
analysis is conducted at the county level, neighboring counties may occasionally exhibit sharp local contrasts due to
administrative boundary granularity and heterogeneous domain indicators. Decision-makers should therefore interpret
localized ranking differences with appropriate caution.
Despite the strengths discussed above, some limitations remain. The current framework relies on manually designed
prompts for LLM-based agents, making the results sensitive to prompt formulation and potentially affecting consistency
across different runs and settings. Future work could address this limitation by exploring automatic prompt tuning
techniques to improve the robustness and consistency of LLM-based agents. By adapting prompts based on data
characteristics and task requirements, the framework can achieve more stable and reliable performance across different
settings.
Overall, AutoMARS provides a unified and extensible framework for automated, multi-domain risk assessment
in complex supply chain environments. By shifting from score-based evaluation to comparison-based reasoning, the
approach improves the robustness and interpretability of risk assessment outcomes, particularly in settings involving
LLM-based analytical processes. The proposed framework demonstrates the potential of multi-agent LLM systems to
support scalable and structured decision-making, offering a practical foundation for risk-aware location evaluation in
real-world supply chain applications.
