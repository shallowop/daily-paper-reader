## 摘要
为分配公共基础设施（如机场起降时刻和能源资源）设计最优拍卖具有挑战性，原因在于资源之间相互依赖且存在复杂的附加约束。尽管 Vickrey-Clarke-Groves（VCG）拍卖能够保证最大福利，但它无法最大化收益，也难以兼顾公共基础设施系统中固有的多目标权衡。我们提出了一种可微、约束感知的组合拍卖设计框架，可对分数可行且近似策略防真的机制进行端到端学习。我们的主要贡献是一种组合约束强制技术，该技术通过构造保证分数可行性，从而避免基于惩罚的近似方法。我们引入了 CAGraph，这是一种新颖的基于图的架构，旨在捕捉物品与竞标者之间的依赖关系，同时将 CANet 和 CAFormer 作为组合设置下的深度学习基线。我们还提供了一种受匹配追踪启发的分解方法，将分数分配映射为可行的离散分配，并实证评估由此产生的离散化差距。在合成基准和机场时刻剥离案例研究中，学习得到的机制在较小的经验遗憾下，其期望收益分别比 VCG 高出至多 73.5% 和 180%。最后，我们可视化了收益、福利与公平的帕累托前沿，为监管者提供决策支持工具，以设计兼顾盈利能力、效率与公平的基础设施拍卖。

## Abstract
Designing optimal auctions for allocating public infrastructure-such as airport slots and energy resources-is challenging due to interdependent resources and complex side-constraints. While the Vickrey-Clarke-Groves (VCG) auction guarantees maximum welfare, it cannot maximize revenue or accommodate the multi-objective trade-offs inherent in public infrastructure systems. We propose a differentiable, constraint-aware framework for combinatorial auction design that enables end-to-end learning of fractionally feasible, approximately strategy-proof mechanisms. Our primary contribution is a combinatorial constraint enforcement technique that ensures fractional feasibility by construction, bypassing the need for penalty-based approximations. We introduce CAGraph, a novel graph-based architecture designed to capture dependencies between items and bidders, alongside CANet and CAFormer as deep learning baselines, for combinatorial settings. We also provide a Matching Pursuit-inspired decomposition that maps fractional allocations to feasible discrete allocations and empirically evaluates the resulting discretization gap. In synthetic benchmarks and an airport slot-divestiture case study, respectively, the learned mechanisms achieve up to 73.5% and 180% higher expected revenue than VCG, under small empirical regret. Finally, we visualize the Pareto frontiers for revenue, welfare, and fairness, providing regulators with a decision-support tool to design infrastructure auctions that balance profitability, efficiency and equity.
