## Abstract 

Time series forecasting supports decision-making in web applica-tions such as online retail and network management, where planned actions can reshape future dynamics. World models provide a natu-ral paradigm for predicting such action-dependent changes beyond extrapolating historical patterns. However, existing time series world models (TSWMs) typically rely on fixed, domain-specific covariate schemas that limit flexible intervention specification, while multimodal forecasters treat text primarily as passive predic-tive context. To investigate these gaps, we first introduce TSWM-Bench , a cross-domain benchmark for multimodal TSWMs span-ning seven real-world datasets, with textual interventions grounded in recorded action–outcome data. Our evaluation reveals that con-ventional multimodal forecasters underperform history-only time series foundation models (TSFMs) despite dataset-specific train-ing, whereas simple text-prefix adaptations improve forecasting across all evaluated TSFM backbones. These findings suggest that pretrained TSFMs encode implicit world knowledge of temporal dynamics, offering a strong foundation for action-conditioned mod-eling beyond conventional multimodal fusion. Building on this insight, we further propose TimeWorld , a plug-in framework that adapts pretrained TSFMs through explicit action-response model-ing. Specifically, the Prototype-Guided Latent Modulation (PLM) module grounds textual actions in temporal dynamics by compos-ing representative response prototypes to modulate future repre-sentations based on the action and historical patterns. The Action Contrastive Learning (ACL) component strengthens action-specific response modeling by contrasting these updates under alterna-tive interventions sharing the same history, distinguishing action types while preserving relative intensity relationships. Experiments across diverse domains and TSFM backbones demonstrate consis-tent improvements over strong baselines.

## 1 Introduction 

Time series forecasting (TSF) underpins web-related applications such as retail planning [33], network management [18], and finan-cial analytics [49]. Recent time series foundation models (TSFMs) have further improved forecasting by learning transferable priors over temporal dynamics through large-scale pretraining [8]. How-ever, forecasting in these applications often requires anticipating how planned actions alter future trajectories, beyond extrapolat-ing historical patterns [27]. For example, an online retailer often needs to estimate demand under alternative discount campaigns before allocating its promotional budget. Motivated by the world-model paradigm of predicting system evolution under different actions [5, 12], we study action-conditioned time series world models 

(TSWMs) that forecast future trajectories from historical observa-tions and a specified action [46]. Existing TSWMs commonly encode interventions as numerical or categorical covariates [27, 32, 46]. Such predefined, domain-specific schemas struggle to capture complex intervention seman-tics ( e.g. , buy-one-get-one offers), limiting cross-domain general-ization [35]. Moreover, their reliance on stepwise future controls makes it difficult to express high-level temporal intervention pat-terns ( e.g. , phasing out discounts) without prescribing exact control sequences. Instead, natural language provides a flexible interface to specify action semantics, intensity, and temporal scope without fixed schemas or stepwise controls [15]. However, recent studies of multimodal time series forecasting typically use text to describe temporal patterns [9, 20], provide domain metadata [16, 22], or sup-ply contextual signals such as news and events [21, 38–40]. In these methods, text provides predictive context rather than specifying arXiv:2610.34797v1 [cs.LG] 26 Oct 2026 Conference acronym ’XX, June 03–05, 2018, Woodstock, NY Hang Ni, Weijia Zhang, & Hao Liu 

interventions whose effects must be modeled. We therefore investi-gate multimodal TSWMs that condition future temporal dynamics on textual actions. Early efforts in multimodal TSWMs rely on synthetic data for counterfactual ground truth [11] or limit evaluation to a single do-main [44]. To address these gaps, we first introduce TSWM-Bench ,a benchmark for multimodal TSWMs spanning seven real-world datasets across economic, physical, and healthcare domains, with interventions ranging from price adjustments to medical treatments. Each instance pairs a univariate history and its observed future with textual action and context descriptions derived from source records. Table 1 compares its coverage with existing benchmarks. We evaluate history-only TSFMs, representative multimodal fore-casters, and covariate-aware TSWMs to examine their capabilities for action-conditioned prediction. Our results reveal that exist-ing multimodal forecasting methods consistently underperform history-only TSFMs despite dataset-specific training. By contrast, simple text-prefix adaptations of TSFMs improve forecasting accu-racy across all evaluated backbones and even outperform covariate-aware TSWMs. These findings motivate a new route to world mod-eling: reusing pretrained world-dynamics priors while learning how textual actions reshape future states .However, effectively adapting pretrained TSFMs to multimodal TSWMs presents two critical challenges: (1) Action Grounding. Incor-porating textual actions requires explicitly translating intervention semantics into state-dependent changes to the predictive dynam-ics encoded by pretrained TSFMs. Existing multimodal forecasters typically fuse textual and temporal representations directly, compli-cating cross-modal interpretation [30] while leaving fine-grained action effects on local temporal patterns implicit. Consequently, cap-turing meaningful intervention responses and their state-dependent evolution remains challenging. (2) Action Discrimination. Even with action grounding, forecasts may remain dominated by pretrained dynamics, obscuring action-specific responses. Forecasting loss alone provides limited supervision for action–response alignment, as it evaluates factual outcomes without comparing responses to alternative actions under the same history. This requires additional supervision for action discrimination to ensure that predictions faithfully reflect the specified intervention. To address these challenges, we further propose TimeWorld ,a plug-in framework for building action-conditioned time series world models from pretrained TSFMs. For action grounding, Prototype-Guided Latent Modulation (PLM) constructs a bank of representative temporal response prototypes and adaptively composes them ac-cording to the specified action, forecast interval, and temporal state. The resulting responses modulate future hidden states within a compact latent subspace, preserving the pretrained temporal com-ponents. For action discrimination, Action Contrastive Learning (ACL) uses large language models (LLMs) to construct alternative interventions under the same history and applies contrastive su-pervision to their latent updates. This strengthens action–response alignment while preserving relative intensity relationships. Context-enhanced Temporal Modeling (CTM) further incorporates textual system context through lightweight prefix conditioning. Exten-sive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness and generality of TimeWorld across diverse domains and TSFM backbones.

## 2 Related Work 

In this section, we review time series foundation models, multi-modal forecasting, and time series world models. 

Time Series Foundation Models. Large-scale pretraining en-ables TSFMs to acquire transferable world knowledge of temporal evolution across datasets [8, 29]. Existing approaches primarily follow two forecasting paradigms. Autoregressive models generate future patches sequentially, allowing flexible prediction horizons but potentially accumulating errors over long rollouts [19, 23, 34]. 

Masked-future models instead predict future patches jointly from historical observations and masked placeholders, enabling parallel inference without recursive error propagation [1, 14, 17]. Despite this implicit knowledge of system dynamics, these models lack mechanisms to ground textual actions in state-dependent changes to future trajectories. 

Multimodal Time Series Forecasting. Multimodal forecasting enriches numerical observations with textual context, exploiting complementary semantic information to improve predictive accu-racy. Text serves varied roles: early efforts use task descriptions 

to formulate forecasting as language generation [42], summarize 

temporal patterns to expose numerical structure [9, 20], or provide 

domain metadata to contextualize heterogeneous series [16, 22, 50]. Beyond describing the task or series, predictive context supplies supplementary information correlated with future observations but unavailable in numerical histories, including retrospective reports and news [21, 38], event announcements [48], and prospective conditions or constraints [11, 39]. Multimodal foundation mod-els further learn transferable numerical–textual representations through shared pretraining, extending context-aware forecasting across datasets [6, 7, 36, 40]. However, predominantly using text TimeWorld: Towards Action-Conditioned Multimodal World Models for Time Series Forecasting Conference acronym ’XX, June 03–05, 2018, Woodstock, NY 

as passive predictive context leaves the causal effects of actively planned interventions insufficiently modeled, motivating the shift toward action-conditioned simulation. 

Time Series World Models. World models for control [12] and interactive video generation [5] motivate modeling temporal evolu-tion for prediction and planning. Inspired by video joint-embedding prediction [2], latent predictive modeling forecasts future represen-tations before decoding observations, but does not condition state transitions on actions [24, 43]. Although multimodal extensions incorporate contextual information [7, 52], they still do not ex-plicitly model how controllable actions alter temporal state tran-sitions. In contrast, early action-conditioned approaches use struc-tured treatments for counterfactual prediction or clinical policy learning [26, 37, 41]. Recent TSWMs encode actions as numeri-cal or categorical covariates: base-station power and antenna set-tings [32], advertising budgets [27], or engineering controls and treatment doses [46]. These formulations restrict actions to domain-specific schemas and may omit intervention details. Language offers a flexible interface without prescribing a unique control sequence, motivating text-conditioned multimodal TSWMs . For example, TAD-iff [11] forecasts under alternative textual conditions through text-attributive diffusion, but its real-data evaluation concerns descrip-tive conditions and semantic consistency rather than recorded ac-tion effects, with ground-truth counterfactual trajectories confined to synthetic data. ReasonCast [44] translates textual actions into semantic interventions through agentic reasoning, but focuses on e-commerce demand. A comprehensive multimodal TSWM bench-mark grounded in real action–outcome records across domains remains missing. We fill this gap with TSWM-Bench and introduce TimeWorld, which extends pretrained predictive dynamics to tex-tual interventions through prototype-guided latent updates and contrastive discrimination.

## 3.3 Evaluation Protocol 

Baselines. We evaluate four classes of baselines, covering zero-shot forecasting and dataset-specific training: (1) Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) : TimesFM 3.0 [14], Chronos-2 [1], Toto 2.0 [17], and Moirai 2.0 [19] provide zero-shot forecasts from numerical histories, establishing the performance of pretrained temporal mod-els without action information. (2) Multimodal Time Series Models :Time-LLM [16], MM-TSFlib from Time-MMD [21], TimeCMA [20], and TADiff [11] are trained on each dataset to evaluate task-specific numerical–textual integration for action-conditioned forecasting. (3) Multimodal TSFMs : ChatTime [36] and Aurora [40] are evaluated zero-shot to assess whether pretrained multimodal representations Conference acronym ’XX, June 03–05, 2018, Woodstock, NY Hang Ni, Weijia Zhang, & Hao Liu 

transfer to textual action conditioning. (4) Covariate-aware TSWM :CovTSWM (our name for the method in [46] ) is trained on each dataset with an adapted covariate schema that encodes recorded actions as structured controls. We use the best-performing mod-ule combinations for the modular frameworks: MM-TSFlib and CovTSWM, with implementation details in Appendix A. All text-capable baselines receive equivalent context and action information through their respective input interfaces. 

Metrics. We report normalized mean absolute error (nMAE) and normalized root mean squared error (nRMSE), scaled by the stan-dard deviation of each input history, and mean absolute scaled error (MASE), scaled by its mean absolute lag-one difference, with lower values indicating better forecasts. All scales use observed historical values only, and zero-scale instances are excluded rather than assigned zero error (Appendix B). Additional implementation details appear in Section 5.1. 

## 3.4 Benchmark Findings 

Table 2 shows that both dataset-trained multimodal models and zero-shot multimodal foundation models have higher aggregate errors than history-only TSFMs, suggesting that access to textual actions alone does not ensure effective forecasting. CovTSWM achieves lower errors than these multimodal baselines across all three metrics, supporting the effectiveness of explicit action model-ing. Yet it remains less accurate than the strongest TSFMs, under-scoring the predictive value of pretrained world-dynamics knowl-edge. These findings raise a central question for world modeling: 

can pretrained TSFMs build on their implicit world dynamics to ex-plicitly model how textual actions shape future trajectories ?We thereby introduce TSFM++, a dataset-specific adaptation applicable to each evaluated TSFM that prepends projected context and action embeddings to its time series tokens (illustrated in Sec-tion 4.1). We denote backbone-specific variants by TSFM++ / backbone .As shown in Table 2, action-prefix adaptation generally reduces aggregate errors, although the gains vary across backbones and Toto 2.0 does not improve in nMAE. These results suggest that textual actions can refine pretrained predictive dynamics, moti-vating TimeWorld’s explicit action-dependent latent updates, as detailed in Section 4. Additional results in Appendix F highlight the advantage of textual action conditioning, with TSFM++ out-performing TimesFM 3.0 using native structured covariates under both zero-shot and fine-tuning settings. 

## 4 TimeWorld 

Figure 1 illustrates the overall architecture of TimeWorld. First, 

Context-enhanced Temporal Modeling (CTM) incorporates textual context into a pretrained TSFM to obtain context-aware temporal representations. Next, Prototype-Guided Latent Modulation (PLM) 

learns a bank of temporal response prototypes and adaptively uses them to update the TSFM hidden states according to the speci-fied action. The original TSFM prediction head then decodes these action-adjusted states into future values. To complement the fore-casting loss, Action Contrastive Learning (ACL) further guides the latent updates to distinguish action types and preserve the ordering of action intensities. 

## 4.1 Context-enhanced Temporal Modeling 

To leverage implicit world knowledge of temporal dynamics, we build TimeWorld upon a pretrained TSFM consisting of three main components: a patch encoder Enc, a temporal backbone Backbone, and a prediction head Dec. Given a historical time series x of length 𝐿 , the patch encoder divides it into patches of length 𝐿 in and maps them into embeddings 

E = Enc (x) ∈ R𝑁 ×𝐷 , where 𝑁 = ⌈𝐿 /𝐿 in ⌉ is the number of input patches and 𝐷 is the hidden dimension. To capture domain-specific dynamics that may not be identifiable from numerical observations alone, we additionally introduce textual context 𝑐 . Specifically, a pretrained text embedding model TextEnc 𝑐 encodes 𝑐 into a vector, which is projected into the TSFM hidden space by a lightweight adapter Adapt 𝑐 comprising two linear layers and a GELU activa-tion: c = Adapt 𝑐 (TextEnc 𝑐 (𝑐 )) ∈ R𝐷 . We prepend this context embedding as an additional token to the numerical patch sequence, obtaining E𝑐 = [c; E] ∈ R(𝑁 +1) × 𝐷 , which is processed by the tem-poral backbone using its native forecasting mechanism. For a forecasting horizon of 𝐻 time steps, the TSFM predicts 𝑁 f =

⌈𝐻 /𝐿 out ⌉ future patches of length 𝐿 out , each associated with a hid-den state g𝑘 ∈ R𝐷 , where 𝑘 = 1, . . . , 𝑁 f indexes future patches. Dif-ferent TSFM architectures obtain these states through distinct fore-casting paradigms. Autoregressive models generate future patches sequentially, using previously predicted patches to compute the next hidden state: g𝑘 = Backbone ([ E𝑐 ; Enc (by<𝑘 )]) last , where by<𝑘 

denotes the preceding predicted patches and the subscript last selects the final hidden state. In contrast, masked-future models obtain all future hidden states jointly by appending 𝑁 f masked tokens Emask ∈ R𝑁 f ×𝐷 , which is formulated as [g1; . . . ; g𝑁 f ] =

Backbone ([ E𝑐 ; Emask ]) future , where future selects the hidden states at the masked future positions. In either paradigm, the TSFM yields context-aware but action-unaware future states g𝑘 , which our sub-sequent PLM module updates into action-conditioned states eg𝑘 .They are finally decoded by the prediction head as by𝑘 = Dec (eg𝑘 ).For comparison, TSFM++, the action-prefix baseline in Section 3.4, directly prepends adapted embeddings of both context and action to the numerical patch sequence, without PLM or ACL modules.

4.2.2 Patch-wise Modulation. To preserve pretrained knowledge of temporal evolution while accommodating action-induced devia-tions, we constrain latent updates to an 𝑅 -dimensional learnable subspace. This subspace is spanned by an orthonormal basis matrix

## 4.3 Action Contrastive Learning 

While PLM achieves fine-grained action grounding, learning from factual actions alone may still be dominated by history patterns and overlook the specific characteristics of the given action. To strengthen action sensitivity, ACL explicitly contrasts the latent up-dates induced by alternative actions under the same history and con-text. It consists of two components: (1) Counterfactual Action Con-struction , which generates variations in action type and intensity, and (2) Contrastive Supervision , which distinguishes action-specific responses and preserves their relative intensity relationships.

## 5.3 Ablation Study 

In this section, we evaluate nine variants to isolate the contribu-tions of PLM and ACL . Within PLM, “w/o Proto.” directly modulates the subspace coordinates without prototypes, “w/ Learn. Proto.” replaces encoded prototypes with randomly initialized learnable vectors, “w/o Ortho.” replaces the orthogonal subspace projections with MLP mappings, “w/o Affine” removes the final scale and shift, and “w/o Seq.” replaces token-level action attention with a sin-gle action embedding. “w/o PLM” uses action-prefix conditioning instead of latent modulation, while “w/o Type”, “w/o Rank”, and “w/o ACL” remove type discrimination, intensity ranking, and both contrastive objectives, respectively. Figure 2 yields three observa-tions: (1) Structured latent modulation improves action grounding. 

Removing prototypes, affine adjustment, or token-level retrieval increases nMAE for every backbone, as does replacing PLM with action-prefix conditioning. Learnable prototypes also worsen all four backbones, supporting the value of temporal response priors encoded by pretrained TSFMs. These results support composing temporal response patterns and adapting them to the action and current state. (2) The subspace parameterization matters, but its ef-fect depends on the backbone. Switching it degrades TimesFM 3.0, Chronos-2, and Toto 2.0, while Moirai 2.0 changes only marginally in the opposite direction. (3) Both contrastive objectives contribute to forecasting accuracy. Removing either objective or ACL as a whole worsens all four backbones, supporting complementary supervision of action type and intensity. 

## 5.4 Parameter Analysis 

In this section, we examine the contrastive weights 𝜆 t and 𝜆 s, which balance action discrimination against forecasting accuracy, and the subspace dimension 𝑅 , which controls the capacity of latent modulation. Each contrastive weight is varied independently over 

{0.001 , 0.003 , 0.01 , 0.03 , 0.1}, and 𝑅 over {8, 16 , 32 , 64 }, keeping the remaining settings fixed. Figure 3 shows that: (1) Moderate type weights provide stable performance, but excessive weighting can be harmful. TimesFM is relatively stable up to 𝜆 t = 0.03, whereas increasing it to 0.1 raises nMAE by 16.7% relative to 0.01. This may reflect overly strong pressure to separate action types at the ex-pense of factual prediction. (2) Performance is particularly robust to the intensity weight. Across the full range of 𝜆 s, each backbone’s nMAE remains within 1.5% of its best value, indicating limited sensitivity to the strength of rank supervision. (3) An intermedi-ate subspace dimension balances capacity and constraint. All four backbones achieve their lowest nMAE at 𝑅 = 32, supporting our chosen dimension. Smaller subspaces may restrict useful action-dependent updates, while larger ones permit unnecessary changes to pretrained representations.

## 6 Conclusion 

In this work, we study time series world models that specify inter-ventions through textual actions beyond fixed covariate schemas, and introduce TSWM-Bench for evaluation across seven real-world datasets. Our benchmark findings suggest that pretrained TSFMs encode implicit world knowledge of temporal evolution, provid-ing a foundation for action-conditioned world modeling. Building on this insight, we propose TimeWorld, a plug-in framework that adapts pretrained TSFMs through explicit action grounding and dis-crimination. Prototype-guided latent modulation translates textual TimeWorld: Towards Action-Conditioned Multimodal World Models for Time Series Forecasting Conference acronym ’XX, June 03–05, 2018, Woodstock, NY 

actions into state- and interval-dependent updates, while action contrastive learning distinguishes responses to different action types and preserves relative intensity relationships under shared histories. Experiments across four TSFM backbones demonstrate consistent improvements in aggregate forecasting accuracy over history-only and action-prefix baselines. Ablation and parameter analyses substantiate the effectiveness of latent modulation and contrastive supervision, while action consistency analysis further reveals stronger action-type discrimination and more coherent intensity-dependent responses.

## A Baseline Implementation Details 

MM-TSFlib [21] uses iTransformer for temporal modeling and GPT-2 for text encoding. CovTSWM [46] combines TiDE with a frozen autoencoder and output gating to condition forecasts on structured actions. We adapt its covariate schema to each dataset’s recorded controls and align their values, magnitudes, and event indicators with the forecast timeline. For FOMC, these covariates encode the explicitly announced target interest rate when available.

## C Counterfactual Action Construction Details 

Each generation request to the LLM includes the factual action and specifies which attribute may change, so type alternatives vary the operation while intensity alternatives retain the operation and follow the assigned magnitude ordering. All candidates preserve unrelated context, units, and temporal information to keep the con-trast focused on the intended action variation. The consistent and regular action descriptions in TSWM-Bench facilitate checks of format, required fields, and the requested variations. Programmatic validation of all generated actions, supplemented by manual inspec-tion of sampled examples, revealed no formatting errors. However, handling irregular, unconstrained action text remains a nontriv-ial challenge beyond the scope of this paper, motivating future benchmarks and solutions for such inputs.

## E Dataset Details 

The history lengths and forecast horizons reflect each domain’s sampling frequency and action timescale, as summarized in Table 5. 

M5. M5 [25] contains daily item–store sales and weekly average prices. Actions describe recorded price changes, including the previ-ous and updated prices and their effective interval. We use 𝐿 = 112 days and 𝐻 ∈ { 7, 28 } days to capture weekly and four-week demand responses, with a fixed 1% sample shared by all methods. 

MOF. MOF [28] pairs Japan’s foreign-exchange interventions with yen-per-dollar observations from FRED. Actions specify the traded currencies, direction, and amount, combining operations on the same date. We use 𝐿 = 60 market observations and 𝐻 ∈ { 1, 5} to ex-amine immediate and short-term responses, aligning observations to separate pre-intervention history from subsequent outcomes. 

FOMC. FOMC [4] pairs monetary policy statements with U.S. two-year Treasury yields. The full statement serves as the textual action, retaining policy information beyond the announced rate. We use 

𝐿 = 60 and 𝐻 ∈ { 1, 5} market observations, with targets beginning after the announcement date. 

Causal Chambers. Causal Chambers [10] provides controlled wind-tunnel experiments that vary inlet fan load while holding other actuators fixed. We forecast downstream absolute pressure with (𝐿, 𝐻 ) = (100 , 100 ). Lengths count native observations because sampling intervals are irregular. 

DaISy. The DaISy heat-exchanger data [3] record liquid flow and outlet temperature every second. We use 𝐿 ∈ { 120 , 300 } and 𝐻 = 1, reflecting two or five minutes of history and the per-step changes in flow. The target is the temperature at the current input step, with history ending at the preceding step. 

Shanghai Diabetes. Shanghai Diabetes [51] combines 15-minute glucose measurements with records of meals, medication, and in-sulin administration. We consolidate simultaneous actions while preserving food quantities and medication details. We use 𝐿 = 96 and 𝐻 ∈ { 4, 8}, corresponding to a 24-hour history and forecast horizons of one or two hours. History excludes the glucose mea-surement contemporaneous with the action. 

HUPA-UCM. HUPA-UCM [13] records glucose, carbohydrate in-take, and insulin doses for people with type 1 diabetes. We use the raw FreeStyle historical glucose records at approximately 15-minute intervals, rather than the released 5-minute interpolated series. We set 𝐿 = 96 (approximately 24 hours) and 𝐻 ∈ { 4, 8}. We exclude instances with long sampling gaps and participants whose pump records cannot be parsed consistently.

## G TSFM Fine-Tuning Study 

We investigate whether fine-tuning the TSFM improves forecasting after action conditioning has been learned. The first stage trains TimeWorld with a frozen TimesFM 3.0 backbone. Starting from the selected checkpoint, the second stage freezes the learned action-conditioning modules and either fine-tunes all TSFM parameters or tunes only the output head and rank-8 attention-layer LoRA. Both variants minimize factual forecasting loss at a learning rate of 10 −6

for up to ten epochs, stopping after three epochs without valida-tion improvement. As shown in Table 7, both strategies slightly increase all three aggregate errors, with a larger degradation un-der full fine-tuning. These results suggest that adapting pretrained TSFM parameters to action-dependent dynamics remains a non-trivial challenge, motivating fine-tuning strategies that better align temporal knowledge with action effects.
