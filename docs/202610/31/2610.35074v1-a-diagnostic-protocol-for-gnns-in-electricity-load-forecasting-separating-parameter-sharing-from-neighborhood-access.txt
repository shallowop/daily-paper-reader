Abstract. A graph neural network (GNN) forecasting a collection of load series combines two mechanisms, sharing parameters across series and reading neighboring series, and a gain over a non-graph model may come from either; telling them apart decides whether a graph is worth maintaining. We propose a model diagnostic protocol that compares the GNN with three reference models: a per-node network, a network shared across nodes without neighbors, and the same GNN without edges. Degree-preserving rewiring tests whether neighbor identity matters. After a tuned benchmark on regional load in France and on low-voltage feeders in the UK, we apply the protocol to deliberately short node histories, as when non-stationary demand leaves only recent observations relevant: the feeders, within one week, and a public dataset of client consumption series. On the feeders, sharing parameters recovers most of the error gap between node-specific models and GNNs, about two thirds against a regularized one. Adding the edges to an otherwise identical backbone brings a smaller gain, half or more of which disappears under rewiring. On the client series, the neighbor mean degrades the per-node model on the shorter window and the edges’ gain is not consistently distinguishable from zero; on the regions of France, where each node’s own history is already informative, the ranking between per-node models and GNNs changes with tuning. A synthetic study points to noisy local measurements as one regime where neighbors help. In an exploratory online aggregation, graph and non-graph forecasters appear complementary, and the weight assigned to GNNs is not proportional to their contribution. In none of the three datasets do the edges account for most of a GNN’s advantage over node-specific models, so a forecasting gain should be credited to the graph only after comparison with per-node and shared references. 

Keywords. Graph neural networks, Model diagnostic protocol, Electricity load fore-casting, Spatio-temporal models, Local and global forecasting, Parameter sharing 

# 1 Introduction 

Electricity demand is measured across many places at once, regions or individual feeders, and these measurements are not independent: weather, calendars, and economic activity register on 

1arXiv:2610.35074v1 [cs.AI] 26 Oct 2026 A Diagnostic Protocol for GNNs in Load Forecasting Campagne et al. 

neighboring meters together. Disaggregated load data are increasingly available (Obst et al. 2021, de Vilmarest and Goude 2021), and hierarchical methods already turn them into gains on aggregate demand (Brégère and Huard 2022, Antoniadis et al. 2024). A graph neural network (GNN) adds this spatial axis to a temporal model: one shared local rule explains each node through its own features and also those of its neighbors, so a single model covers a whole set of regions or feeders. On the half-hourly datasets, the problem is to forecast the next day at every node from a window of past observations. In practice, the demand graph is not given: electrical topology, geographic proximity, and statistical similarity each suggest a different node adjacency, so building a graph is part of the modeling. A second decision is the choice of reference model. Beating a model fitted to national demand does not isolate the contribution of edges: the conclusion depends on choosing the right reference model. Any such comparison assesses the value of a prescribed node adjacency under the chosen model classes, so a negative result concerns those choices only. The question pursued in this article is therefore when a prescribed node adjacency is informative enough to earn the cost of building and maintaining it, and how much of a GNN’s gain over node-specific models is already brought by parameter sharing alone. 

Contributions. We propose a diagnostic protocol, built over existing architectures, that credits a forecasting gain to a candidate node adjacency only once what parameter sharing already provides has been set aside. The protocol is designed for a practitioner who must decide whether a graph is worth deploying and maintaining. It sets three reference models against the GNN: i) a model fitted separately to each node from the same local covariates, which gives the total gap a graph has to justify; ii) a network shared across nodes on the identity adjacency, without access to neighbors, which measures the edges conditional on sharing; and iii) the GNN itself with its edges removed, which separates the edges from the change of backbone. Comparing these models along both orders of introducing sharing and neighborhood access decomposes the error gap, up to the architectural differences between the MLPs and the GNN, and rewiring the graph (while keeping the degrees unchanged) assesses whether the identity of the neighbors matters beyond their number. We work with three load datasets spanning geographic regions ( regions-fr ), feeders ( weave-uk ), and individual clients ( electricity ), and a synthetic study where the conditions are controlled. We run the protocol in full on weave-uk and electricity , where we deliberately keep each node’s history short, and answer four questions. (Q1) Does a GNN forecast load better than strong non-graph models? It depends on the dataset: in a tuned benchmark of 8 GNN architectures against statistical, boosting, foundation-model, and persistence baselines, no GNN beats a pooled gradient-boosted model on regions-fr 1,whereas on weave-uk only 2 of the 8 GNNs have a lower mean RMSE than the best baseline, the zero-shot foundation model t0-gamma, both by less than one seed std. (Q2) Where a GNN wins, does the gain come from the graph? On weave-uk , mostly not: sharing parameters across nodes explains most of it. (Q3) At equal architecture, do neighbors help? A little: against the same backbone without edges, the edges add a small consistent gain on weave-uk in the larger-dataset comparison, half or more of which the tested rewirings remove; on electricity the gain is not consistently different from zero once seed variability is included. (Q4) When do neighbors help? The synthetic study identifies noisy local measurements as one mechanism. On real data, their contribution depends on the dataset and the window, and the protocol measures it case by case. # 2 Related Work 

Generalized additive models remain the operational instrument for conditional-mean load forecasting (Fan and Hyndman 2010, 2012, Pierrot and Goude 2011), with quantile and location-scale extensions for the full distribution (Fasiolo et al. 2021, Gilbert et al. 2023). Their error grows under structural breaks: the Covid-19 crisis (Jiang et al. 2021) and the European price surge (Doumèche et al. 2026) are relevant recent cases, which motivated adaptive variants coupling online learning with filtering (de Vilmarest et al. 2024). These models address an aggregated signal, whereas decentralization produces spatially distributed ones (Williams and Short 2020). Recurrent architectures capture the temporal dependence of load series (Shi et al. 2018, Marino et al. 2016, Massaoudi et al. 2021) without representing relations between nodes, which is the gap GNNs fill; they have carried spatio-temporal forecasting in traffic (Guo et al. 2019, Chen et al. 2020) and, more recently, in power systems (Jiang et al. 2023, 2024). Two lines bear directly on the comparisons run here. MTGNN (Wu et al. 2020) and ADLGNN (Sriramulu et al. 2023) learn the node adjacency from the data instead of receiving it, and both report electricity benchmarks. The Appendix evaluates MTGNN under the same data splits and training protocol as the larger-dataset comparison. Attention and multi-scale variants pursue the same end by other means, coupling graph attention with recurrence and physical line attributes (Orji et al. 2025), filtering in the frequency domain (Mo et al. 2025), or updating the topology from temporal context (Zhu et al. 2025). Alongside them, pretrained forecasters now enter the same benchmarks, PriceFM (Yu et al. 2025) embedding a graph bias for European price forecasting and the broader family surveyed by Ferdaus et al. (2026), which is why the benchmark of Section 6.1 carries foundation models among its baselines. Hybrid designs pursue a related goal from the statistical side, Neural Additive Models (Agarwal et al. 2021) and their probabilistic variants (Thielmann et al. 2024) carrying the feature-wise decomposition of a GAM inside a neural architecture. Comparisons with aggregated or global non-graph models do not, by themselves, distinguish the value of sharing parameters from that of accessing neighbors. Closest to this question, Montero-Manso and Hyndman (2021) contrast local and global models for groups of series, Shao et al. (2022) show that node identity embeddings let a plain multilayer perceptron (MLP) rival spatio-temporal GNNs, and Cini et al. (2023) study local and global components in spatio-temporal forecasting. The latter work measures how much node-specific capacity a global model needs, supplied through node-specific parameters and embeddings. We measure which part of the gap between a node-specific model and a GNN the graph explains: we decompose the error gap from a node-specific reference model along both orders of introducing parameter sharing and neighborhood access, including a non-shared model given the neighbor mean. To our knowledge, the cited studies do not report this two-path decomposition.

3.1 GNNs and MLPs: One Family of Node-wise Models 

Each node 𝑖 carries features 𝐱 𝑖 ∈ ℝ 𝑑 , and a model returns a forecast ̂ 𝐲 𝑖 ∈ ℝ ℎ. Models from an independent MLP per region to an attentional GNN differ along three design decisions: i) Parameter sharing : Either use 𝑁 node-specific models, or a single model applied to every node. The latter divides the parameter count by 𝑁 and pools the data of all nodes, at the cost of imposing the same input–output rule on nodes that may differ. ii) Neighborhood access : The support of 𝐀 induces the node adjacency, which marks the nodes whose features are involved in node 𝑖 ’s forecast. With 𝐀 = 𝐈 , only 𝑖 ’s own features are used in its forecast; on the other extreme, 𝐀 = 𝟏𝟏 ⊤ leads to full connectivity; sparse node adjacency lies between those extremes. iii) Neighborhood aggregation : a GNN aggregates neighbors symmetrically, so the aggregation is permutation-invariant, whereas an MLP reading concatenated features gives each node a fixed input slot, which is permutation-sensitive. # 4 Methodology 

The diagnostic protocol takes the graph construction and the GNN architecture as inputs; this section sets out those used here. A GNN is specified by the adjacency it reads and by the convolution that reads it; forecasts from several seeds or architectures are then combined. 

4.1 Graph Construction and Selection 

Typically, the demand graph is not given with the data: nodes are regions or feeders, and an edge must encode a dependence that drives demand, which the electrical wiring explains only in part. The spatial construction kernelizes the geodesic distance, 𝐝(𝑢, 𝑣) = exp{−𝐝𝐢𝐬𝐭(𝑢, 𝑣) 2/𝜎 2𝑏 }, with 𝜎 𝑏 

being here the median pairwise distance. On regions-fr , we keep only the closest pairs, 

(𝑾 𝜆 )𝑢𝑣 = 𝐝(𝑢, 𝑣) ⋅ 1{𝐝(𝑢, 𝑣) ≥ 𝜆}, 𝑢, 𝑣 ∈ , (2) where 𝜆 is the largest level that keeps the graph connected (Williams and Short 2020). As weather systems and regional activity drive demand and are shared by adjacent regions, the idea is to drop the distant pairs that would mostly dilute the neighbor mean, while keeping the graph connected. The considered data-driven constructions use the DTW distance, the Pearson correlation, and the precision matrix of the load series; DistSplines weights an edge by the 𝐿 2 distance between the two nodes’ fitted temperature-to-load splines, and GL3SR (Humbert et al. 2021) learns a smooth graph from the data. The graph is selected based on the validation RMSE. The spatial graph keeps 28 of the 132 ordered pairs of regions. The data-driven constructions are used as computed, DTW keeping 130 pairs and the others all 132 , so a convolution that reads

4.2 Architectures and Aggregation 

Our benchmark covers GCN (Kipf and Welling 2017), GraphSAGE (SAGE) (Hamilton et al. 2017), APPNP (Gasteiger et al. 2019), GAT (Veličković et al. 2018), GATv2 (Brody et al. 2022), Transformer-Conv (Transformer) (Shi et al. 2021), ChebConv (Defferrard et al. 2016) and TAGConv (TAG) (Du et al. 2017), all inside the same residual backbone: a linear node encoder, residual blocks with layer normalization around each convolution, and a linear readout of the 48 outputs. The forecasts of several seeds or architectures are combined either uniformly or by the ML-Poly algorithm (Gaillard et al. 2014), which sets the weights 𝑝 𝑗,𝑡 of the aggregate ̂𝑦 𝑡 = ∑ 𝐾 𝑗=1 𝑝 𝑗,𝑡 𝑥 𝑗,𝑡 from past performance. A seed ensemble, which averages the forecasts of several seeds (Lakshminarayanan et al. 2017), captures the variability due to initialization and optimization only, so the spread across seeds does not measure total predictive uncertainty. 

# 5 Experiments 

Design. The experiments evaluate GNNs at two levels: i) The benchmark level asks how well architecture–graph pairs forecast after tuning, taken 

individually , in seed ensembles , and in mixtures of architectures (Section 6.1). ii) The diagnostic level attributes a gain (Sections 6.2 to 6.4): it fits prescribed model classes under common training rules and varies parameter sharing, neighborhood access and the identity of the neighbors across training volumes and synthetic regimes, the empty-graph GNN separating the edges from the backbone and degree-preserving rewiring separating the identity of the neighbors from their number. Benchmark scores rank forecasters without attributing their gains; diagnostic scores attribute gains within the specified model classes without full hyperparameter tuning, and meet the benchmark only in Section 6.2, where tuning changes the ranking. Outside both levels, a separate exploratory analysis aggregates older GNN configurations online with non-graph forecasters (Section 6.5). Table 1 lists the blocks and their fixed or distinctive settings. 

5.1 Datasets 

regions-fr . The French network operator (RTE) publishes the half-hourly load of the 12 French administrative regions, from which we use the period January 2015 to December 2019 and call this dataset regions-fr . Covariates are calendar variables (time of day, weekday, day type, holidays, position in the year, daylight saving) and weather conditions (temperature, cloud cover, wind) measured at 32 stations and averaged within each region; no weather forecast is used. The target is each region’s load in MW (megawatts), and system-wide scores are computed on their sum. 

weave-uk . This dataset contains the half-hourly smart-meter load of low-voltage feeders, as released by the UK data platform (Weave 2). We use 500 feeders of Scottish and Southern Electricity Networks (Campagne 2026), recorded February 14 –28 , 2024 and selected to have no missing values and to span heterogeneous load profiles. Only calendar variables and lagged load are available.

Tuning. Optuna (Akiba et al. 2019) tunes every architecture with 40 trials scored on the validation RMSE of the summed forecast, the graph construction being searched jointly with the other hyper-parameters; the MLPs run 10 seeds, the gradient-boosted models and GNNs 3. The search spaces, the settings of the untuned baselines and the retained configurations are given in Section H of the Appendix. The diagnostic comparisons use prescribed model classes without full hyperparameter searches, the limited validation-based selections being specified in each block; their widths and backbones are stated there, and common width should not be read as matched total capacity.

6.5 Complementarity of Graph and Non-Graph Forecasters 

An online expert aggregation asks whether graph and non-graph forecasters complement each other 3.On regions-fr , ML-Poly combines the national forecasts of 8 experts: 6 GNNs, a weight-shared feedforward network, and the additive model; each family is withdrawn in turn (Figure B.1 in the Appendix). Forecasting each half-hour from the covariates observed at that half-hour (single-step setting), the mixture reaches 970 MW, 279 MW below its best expert. Withdrawing the GNNs costs 

132 MW and withdrawing the two non-graph experts 117 MW, so each family improves the mixture; removing whole architectures, however, does not isolate the contribution of their edges. On a synthetic control whose nodes depend only on their own covariates, the GNN experts’ share of the mixture’s error falls from 14% to 4% . Weights are not proportional to contribution: on a synthetic dataset forecast a day at a time, whose structure the experts cannot exploit, the GNN experts hold 

0.86 of the weight, above their count share of 0.75 , yet their withdrawal costs 34 MW, 4% of the error. The analysis uses its own split, older configurations, and 1 seed per expert, and two of its GNN experts were selected on its test year, which biases their withdrawal cost upward; its figures are descriptive (Section B). 

# 7 Conclusion 

We proposed a diagnostic protocol that separates the two mechanisms a GNN combines, parameter sharing and neighborhood access, by setting the GNN against a per-node model, a shared model without neighbors, and the empty-graph GNN, and by rewiring the graph at matched degrees. We ran it in full on the weave-uk feeders and the electricity clients, after a tuned benchmark on 

regions-fr and the feeders. (Q1) Does a GNN forecast load better than strong non-graph models? Not uniformly. On regions-fr , no GNN beats a pooled gradient-boosted model, although a bottom-up mixture of the 8 GNNs, at 809 MW, finishes ahead of every individual model of Table 2; against a per-node MLP the ranking changes with tuning, the tuned per-node MLP at 922 MW falling within the 891 –986 MW range of the tuned GNNs, whereas at a fixed configuration it has the lowest mean error at every training volume. On weave-uk , with 9 training days per feeder, the zero-shot t0-gamma is the best baseline and has the lowest MAPE of the benchmark; only GATv2 and APPNP have a lower mean RMSE, both by less than one seed std, and a ChebConv of order one, which reads no neighbor, is level with Chronos-2. The ranking of convolutions does not carry from one dataset to the other. (Q2) Where a GNN wins, does the gain come from the graph? On weave-uk , mostly not. Sharing

Against a per-node ridge, the strongest node-specific reference wherever the gap is nonzero, the shared MLP still recovers 63% and 71% of the gap on weave-uk , although this step also moves from a linear to a nonlinear model; with the model class held fixed, a shared ridge recovers 84% and 41% (Appendix). in the larger-dataset comparison. The empty-graph GNN keeps the backbone and the parameter count of the GNN, so this comparison holds capacity fixed. On weave-uk , the edges improve the per-node error by 0.0086 and 0.0196 , about 6–21% of the sharing increment, and degree-matched rewiring removes half or more of the gain measured with each edge stored once; a shared MLP that reads the neighbor mean is not distinguishable from the GNN there. On electricity , the edge gain stays at about +0.005 or less and is not consistently different from zero once seed variability is included, and it vanishes at 60 days with each edge stored once. (Q4) When do neighbors help? The synthetic study identifies noisy local measurements as one mechanism, without establishing a general boundary between regimes. On real data, their contribution depends on the dataset and the window: the neighbor mean moves the fixed regions-fr per-node MLP from 876 to about 871 MW, recovers 16 –30% of the gap on weave-uk and degrades the per-node model on the 11 -day electricity window, while on the feeders the edge gain at constant backbone stays positive over both windows. A forecasting gain should be credited to the graph only if it survives the comparison with a per-node model, a shared model without neighbors, and the same backbone without edges. 

Limitations and perspectives. In the controlled comparisons, each cell corresponds to the mean of 3 seeds, so differences of the order of the reported dispersion are not claimed as rankings. On the non-shared path, the neighbor mean is a fixed one-hop summary, not the learned aggregation of the GNN, so that path is not matched in architecture. The larger-dataset decomposition is run at one fixed width; its signs hold at widths 32 , 64 , and 128 on weave-uk and in the 11 -day electricity 

cell, whereas the 60 -day cell, whose total includes zero, changes sign across widths (Appendix); configurations tuned model by model are not tested there, and on regions-fr tuning changes the ranking. On weave-uk , the node bootstrap does not cover seasonal or weather variability. The short-window comparison also retains a graph estimated from the longer training record; re-estimating it inside the 30 -hour window gives a more accurate GNN and doubles the edge gain at constant backbone (Appendix). The decomposition is measured on one week of one distribution operator and on five 11 -day windows of the electricity record ending between March and July

The same protocol runs on two synthetic datasets. The generator above produces a temperature field per region and a load obtained by passing it through the region’s own spline. # G Descriptive Analysis of Learned Representations 

The main text compares the graph with its per-node reference model and decomposes the observed gaps. This block describes what the fitted GNNs encode. It does not supply further evidence for the graph’s predictive contribution. In the single-step regression framework, we inspect localized graph explanations; in the sequence-to-sequence setting, we compare latent representations and analyze the attention mechanisms emerging from temporal and spatial dependencies. Beyond forecasting accuracy, a trained model can be inspected with interpretability tools to probe which spatial and feature-level factors it relies on. We treat these tools as a complementary analysis and read their output with caution, since post-hoc explanations remain indicative (Jain and Wallace 2019). 

GNNExplainer. GNNExplainer (Ying et al. 2019) identifies the subgraphs and node features that influence a model’s decision the most. The method optimizes the mutual information between the forecast and a compact explanatory subgraph, distinguishing relevant from irrelevant connections. In practice, this produces a mask over the adjacency matrix and feature space, which reads as the regional links and the covariates used by the prediction. For electricity demand, GNNExplainer indicates which of the links encoded in the input graph a forecast relies on, for instance between climatic zones with similar load behaviors; it cannot reveal a relation that the graph does not contain. 

Attention-based interpretability. Attention mechanisms in GATs assign learnable weights to neighbors during message passing. These coefficients describe how the fitted layer combines its inputs and can vary over time; they are not, by themselves, measures of predictive importance (Jain and Wallace 2019). We first project sequences of attention matrices with principal component analysis (PCA) or uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) to identify recurrent temporal patterns. We then test predictive importance directly by retaining edges according to their attention rank and measuring the resulting forecast error under a fixed downstream model.

On weave-uk , whose 9 training days leave 9 points per half-hour and which has no weather, a single ARIMA-X runs on the half-hourly summed load with daily Fourier terms and a weekend indicator, forecasting 48 steps from each origin with frozen parameters (retained: order (3, 1, 2) , 6 harmonics); its order is selected by AIC on the training days, its design among 3

on the validation days, and it is re-estimated on both before the test. Chronos-2, t0-gamma, and Timestral are used zero-shot under a scheme fixed in advance, one 48 -step median forecast of the summed load at each daily origin. t0-gamma was scored in 2 settings fixed before scoring, without post-processing (reported) and with the 4 adjustments its model card recommends ( 1747 MW and 

149.0 kWh); Timestral keeps its package defaults. Variants run as side information score worse on the test: on weave-uk , one model per feeder ( 224.9 kWh) and the 500 feeders read as one multivariate series ( 198.3 kWh); on regions-fr , the 12 regions read as one multivariate series ( 1769 MW). The MLPs run 10 seeds, the gradient-boosted models and GNNs 3. The retained configurations are listed in Table H.1 of the Appendix. The diagnostic comparisons use prescribed model classes without full hyperparameter searches, the limited validation-based selections being specified in each block. Their widths and backbones are stated in each experimental block; common width should not be read as matched total capacity.
