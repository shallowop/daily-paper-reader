## Abstract 

In recent years, a growing number of online retail companies have embraced deep learning models for demand forecasting, leverag-ing their scalability in processing large-scale datasets. Accurate demand forecasts enable improved planning and allow operational flexibility to adjust sales accordingly. Moreover, quantile estima-tion has gained widespread application as it offers valuable uncer-tainty quantification, thereby enhancing the robustness of decision-making processes. However, limited research has addressed the effective quantification of uncertainty when target labels are am-biguous. In retail, actual sales can be capped by stock and boosted by temporary promotions, whereas the target is underlying de-mand. Such scenarios are characterized by double censoring and no deep-learning pipeline yet exists for such data. In this paper, we introduce a straightforward framework for learning quantiles from doubly-censored observations and propose a tailored evaluation metric. Our approach is supported by theoretical guarantees, and we empirically demonstrate that our metric correlates well with the oracle calibration measure. Experimental results show that our estimator surpasses existing baselines. Finally, inventory simula-tions and real-world applications confirm the practical utility of our method in retail operations.

## 1 Introduction 

In supply chain management, quantile regression is highly val-ued for its distribution-free nature and ability to estimate uncer-tainty. Retail giants like JD and Amazon utilize deep learning-based quantile estimation for inventory optimization[25, 28, 29]. How-ever, when sales data are censored, such as due to stockouts or promotions, quantile regression estimates can become biased. As illustrated in Figure 1 , right-censoring happens with perishable items, where low stock causes frequent stockouts and conceals true demand. On the flip side, left-censoring occurs with promotional products, where temporary promotions lead to observed sales sur-passing actual demand. Daily necessities typically have sufficient stock and rarely go on promotion, so their sales data usually reflects the actual demand. When right and left censoring coexist in one dataset, it’s called doubly censoring. 

Figure 1: Censored data example. 

Using or discarding doubly censored data both distort forecasts: the former fits the model on biased labels, the latter discards usable information[40]. In practice, retailers often revise sales using ad hoc business rules, yet without true demand they cannot verify the accuracy or feed the revised data back into the forecasting pipeline. Consequently, demand forecasting still lacks an end-to-end training method for doubly censored data and an appropriate metric to evaluate models under this scenario. Survival analysis offers a direct parallel: only a lower bound on lifetime is observed, producing right-censored responses. Re-weighting techniques developed for this arXiv:2610.40138v1 [cs.LG] 29 Oct 2026 CIKM ’26, November 07–11, 2026, Rome, Italy Ziwen Ye, Jiawen Wei, Teng Song, Chen Chen, and Guangrui Ma 

setting motivate our unified framework, which trains any deep network end-to-end with the Doubly Portnoy Estimator (DPE). Likewise, while quantile forecasts are usually judged by calibration and sharpness, no calibration test exists for doubly censored data. Borrowing again from survival methodology, we propose Extended D-Calibration to assess the calibration of competing models under double censoring. In conclusion, this paper presents a unified framework for train-ing and evaluating deep models under doubly censored data. Al-though motivated by e-commerce demand forecasting, the frame-work is domain-agnostic and can be extended to any setting with similar censoring patterns. Our contributions are as follows. 

• We introduce an end-to-end algorithm that trains deep mod-els with the DPE, and provide the first formal proof of its properness under doubly censored data, closing a long-standing theoretical gap in quantile estimation with incomplete ob-servations. 

• To evaluate models trained under such censoring, we propose Extended D-Calibration, a new metric for doubly censored data, and prove that it precisely gauges the gap between modelled and true probabilities under mild assumptions. 

• We first validate the proposed metric and algorithm through simulations, then confirm their effectiveness on two large-scale real-world datasets. Inventory simulations further demon-strate their superiority in stock control, and online A/B tests verify their practical impact. 

## 2 Related Work 2.1 Probabilistic Demand Forecasting 

Probabilistic demand forecasting plays a crucial role in inventory management across various sectors. A widely studied problem in this domain is the newsvendor problem, which identifies a spe-cific quantile of the demand distribution as the optimal inventory level[2, 4]. Traditional approaches for estimating demand quan-tiles typically rely on deterministic forecasts and the historical residuals of predicted values[21]. With the rise of deep learning, neural network-based demand forecasting has become increasingly prevalent. For example, to capture uncertainty, DeepAR employs a Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) to generate hidden states and estimates distribution parameters through maximum likelihood estimation[30]. This approach requires assumptions about the un-derlying data distribution. In contrast, quantile regression offers a non-parametric approach for probabilistic forecasting that does not require distributional assumptions[20]. Building on this foundation, Wen, Lim, and other researchers[22, 36] have designed Seq2Seq models that directly estimate quantiles using quantile loss as the loss function. How-ever, these models typically provide predictions for a fixed set of quantiles. To enable arbitrary quantile forecasting, a spline-based neural network have been proposed[5, 11, 25, 32]. These networks leverage the flexibility of splines to interpolate between quantiles, thereby allowing for more granular probabilistic forecasts. In the theoretical analysis of probabilistic forecasting, the theory of scoring rules serves as a fundamental framework. A loss function that qualifies as a proper scoring rule ensures that model training with this function can elicit the true underlying distribution[12]. To evaluate the effectiveness of different proper scoring rules, re-cent studies have widely adopted calibration and sharpness as key evaluation criteria[8, 10, 32]. Our study also employs these metrics to evaluate model performance. 

## 2.2 Censored Quantile Regression 

As highlighted in the introduction, censored data remain largely overlooked in demand forecasting. To exploit such data, we draw upon survival analysis, a field with an extensive literature on cen-soring (see [27, 35] for a comprehensive survey). Deep learning has recently been combined with survival analy-sis under right censoring [19, 40]. Some studies use censored data during training [1, 13]. In the statistical literature, doubly censored data have been studied via quantile regression [18, 23, 27], but few works link censored quantile regression to deep models. The q-rnn package trains networks with left censoring [6]. Pearce et al. extend Portnoy’s Estimator to train deep models via an iterative re-weighting algorithm [26]. Yanagisawa shows that Portnoy’s Esti-mator is proper, so the true distribution is recovered by optimization [39]. On the evaluation side, the C-index [17] quantifies median dis-crimination only, whereas D-Calibration [16] assesses calibration under right censoring. Neither extends to doubly censored data. We close this gap by introducing Extended D-Calibration, the first metric to evaluate calibration in the presence of both left and right censoring.

## 5.4 Real-world Datasets Results 

5.4.1 Real-world Datasets. For real-world experiments, since pub-lic retail datasets such as M5 and Favorita do not collect censoring information, which is prevalent in e-commerce retailing, we col-lected two datasets from two online grocery retailing platforms of Meituan, namely Maicai and Youxuan, covering the period from October 10th to November 15th, 2023. Notably, Maicai and Youxuan have different business models, resulting in distinctly different sales distributions. We aim to demonstrate the generalizability of our method by showing its effectiveness across these different types of distributions. Each dataset is split into three subsets: the initial 30 days are allocated for training, followed by 1 day for valida-tion, and the final 4 days are reserved for testing. Since the data are proprietary due to data privacy concerns, we provide detailed descriptions of our datasets to ensure reproducibility. 

• Youxuan (community group-buying platform): 8.52 million records; 19.84% right-censored, 3.56% left-censored. 

• Maicai (online supermarket platform): 18.07 million records; 18.52% right-censored, 6.26% left-censored. Censoring labels are derived from system logs. Stockouts are right-censored ( Δ = 1, 𝑅 = sales). Promotions are reactive and unplanned; the resulting sales surge is treated as left-censored (Δ = 2, 𝐿 = sales), with the target being baseline demand without promotion. Normal sales are uncensored ( Δ = 0, 𝐿 = 𝑅 = sales). Both datasets share the following features: traffic, sales, and price histories. The data are partitioned as follows: training on days 1–30, validation on day 31, and testing on days 32–35. 

5.4.2 Loss Comparison. Empirical results on synthetic data con-firm that Extended D-Calibration aligns most closely with the oracle calibration score. On the two real-world datasets, where true de-mand is unavailable for censored observations, we rank models solely by Extended D-Calibration and sharpness. As shown in Ta-ble 3 , DPE ranks in the top two for every metric, demonstrating its effectiveness. On Youxuan, DPE ranks second for both metrics. Notably, LogNorm achieves the best calibration but the worst sharp-ness among all models, underscoring that calibration alone is insuf-ficient for model comparison. Censor, it trades a modest worsening in sharpness (10.3 vs. 9.82) for a 70.6% reduction in calibration error (0.05 vs. 0.17). Notably, Excl. ## 6 Conclusion 

This study develops a novel probabilistic framework that explicitly addresses doubly censored data prevalent in e-commerce demand forecasting. Specifically, we propose a deep learning-based quantile regression framework, design the Doubly Portnoy Estimator (DPE), an iterative re-weighting algorithm for training deep models with censored data, and develop Extended D-Calibration, a metric to evaluate models under double censoring. The efficacy of our frame-work is demonstrated via simulation and two real-world datasets. Application results verify that the proposed method effectively mit-igates overstocking and stockout losses in inventory management, thereby improving operational efficiency and profitability.
